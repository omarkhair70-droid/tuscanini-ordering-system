### The human side that must survive the creative work
Restaurant food is **sensory, bodily, social, temporal and local**. Journey begins with hunger/anticipation, seeing open food, choosing, customizing, waiting, carrying or sharing, opening wrapper, first bite and remembering/returning; different visitors have different tempos. Photographs of a worker's gloved hand at human scale, real branded package, people at counter and promotions linked to local football are potentially meaningful *relations* rather than generic lifestyle decoration. Do not invent a staff-hand-off “ritual” or assume an advertised portion is an ordinary delivered portion without observation.

HFI research lens: Gayler / Sas / Kalnikaitė ACM TOCHI 2022 review of 109 papers and 18 chef interviews; food experiences exceed visual taste cues. Transfer the research question, NOT the claim that any such experience has been tested on Tuscanini.

### Seven translated principles (from Pass 04)
1. **FORM × FLAVOR** — Historical item names repeat across crepe/pizza/sandwich/pasta (e.g. تشيكن فاير); possible alternative discovery taxonomy, NOT a claim that recipes are identical or people prefer it.
2. **REVEAL AS PROOF** — Real cuts reveal fillings, but animation should improve comprehension vs a good still.
3. **HUMAN SCALE** — Staff-held food offers a proportion cue; no inflated dimensions.
4. **MATERIAL HANDOFF** — Authentic wrap/box/receipt/handling potentially connects physical & digital; need actual observations.
5. **TWO TEMPOS** — Exploratory hungry visitor and repeat exact-order visitor have different needs; no mandatory cinematic introduction.
6. **WAITING HAS TRUTH** — Saved/pending is not confirmed/preparing; statuses must come from operational events.
7. **SENSORY RESTRAINT** — Sound/texture only when real and useful, optional, muted where appropriate.

### Competing creative engines (research hypothesis, NOT three websites)
- **One Flavor, Many Forms:** Strong in information architecture; recipe equivalence needs proof; not main identity.
- **The Inside Is the Event → INSIDE / OUT:** Strongest observed candidate for photographic/cropping/typographic rhythm; contrast of contained exterior and dense real inside. May be generic among street food; reject if overused or misleading.
- **Made, Held, Handed Over:** Potential bridge between staff, customer, product and packaging; still unobserved as an actual process.

**Synthesis:** Explore Inside/Out visually while keeping the other two as product/physical investigations. The creative idea MUST work differently across brand first encounter, exact product search, offers, cart, status and packaging, or be narrowed. Do NOT turn Inside/Out into an official tagline without creative/user sign-off.

### Research scenarios (DESK STRESS TESTS, not user test results)
- Repeat buyer on weak network, with 40 seconds and a known food name.
- New buyers choosing by taste/budget and needing accurate size/add-on information.
- Busy kitchen receiving different food forms sharing a product nickname.
- Football-promo click seeking exact offer terms rather than a home-page detour.
These expose product truths and constraints, not validated user preferences. Tree testing/card sorting still required before rearchitecting product categories.

### Observations from creative labs
- Lab 06A: the same cropped authentic 640px photo tested as a factual still, editorial composition, and user-controlled detail comparison; editorial contrast richer, zoom exposes source limitations. User judged the later integrated HTML as compelling, *not* a final maximum or merely an image viewer.
- Asset audit: 12 owner-supplied 640×640 photos, not a complete live catalog. True photos are an editorial library; matching each photo to an exact menu item would be speculative.
- Visual Constitution: real food is material; contrast calm/loaded; typography can carry visual weight; honest portions; no repeated giant cutaway hero on every page; motion is additive not required.
- **Implementation lesson:** initial rebirth PR Home dropped real imagery, font roles and prototype interactions. A passing Next.js build does not validate the art direction. The old repo is not the cause; an incomplete design transfer is.
