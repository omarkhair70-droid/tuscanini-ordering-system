## 2 — Three creative engines tested (NOT three site directions)

### Candidate A — ONE FLAVOR, MANY FORMS
- Anchor: identical legacy names across crepe / pizza / pasta / sandwich.
- Can generate: information architecture, exact-item search, cross-recipe comparison, helpful labels.
- Weakness: generic configurator language; identical names do not establish identical recipes. It forces menu ontology to act like brand myth.
- **Decision: retain as a separate product-architecture research lane; not the main brand engine.**
- Kill if: recipes are fundamentally unrelated, or tests show flavor-first repeatedly misleads.

### Candidate B — THE INSIDE IS THE EVENT → refined to **INSIDE / OUT**
- Anchor: direct page screenshots repeatedly expose fillings/sections of handheld food; packaged forms contain interiors visibly different from the exterior.
- What the refined hypothesis adds: a formal contrast between *contained outside* and *layered inside*. The core is not “put a mask reveal everywhere”; it is a worldview about how material is framed, cropped, concealed, exposed, and made legible.
- Can generate: distinctive crop/photographic pairs, macro vs context scale, typography density contrasts, truthful portion proof, controlled animated editorial reveals, packaging research, meaningfully different compositions.
- Weakness: generic fast-food cross-section imagery; brand can become repetitive, exaggerated, or disconnected from non-crepe products.
- **Decision: APPROVE FOR EDITORIAL/CREATIVE EXPLORATION ONLY. Do not lock as unique identity until a prototype and ordinary-serving food photos support it.**
- Kill if: genuine servings look materially unlike promotional pictures, user comprehension is no better than clear stills, or the formal language fails outside a hero/cutaway.

### Candidate C — MADE, HELD, HANDED OVER
- Anchor: staff-held food and physical branded materials in screenshots.
- Can generate: true human scale, shop/staff presence, packaging/receipt/ordering-status continuity.
- Weakness: no observed prep, wrap, queue, carry or handoff; risks inventing a cinematic restaurant ritual.
- **Decision: retain as a prospective physical/service-research lane.**
- Kill if: handoff is ordinary and there is no distinct useful material or behavior to translate.

**Synthesis finding:** B has the strongest directly observed *creative material*. A has clearer *catalog implications*. C could be a bridge to the physical brand, but has the least evidence. This is an ordering of available evidence, NOT a claim about customer preference or sales performance.

## 3 — Working thesis: INSIDE / OUT

> A digital food world built around the contrast between the restrained exterior form and the layered, sometimes visually overwhelming interior of actual Tuscanini food. What is disclosed must be real; what is controlled must be useful.

**Status:** working creative engine, **not** a public tagline, not a logo direction, not an approved final thesis.

**Why it may be productive:** It has a formal property beyond a single object: **opposites** (sealed/open, overview/detail, wrapper/food, held/interior, quiet/full). Such opposites can create different visual rhythms and behaviors across contexts.

**Why it may fail:** Many street-food businesses advertise cut-open food; “inside out” is not inherently proprietary. We still need tests that distinguish a Tuscanini authored language from generic fast-food styling. Do not add proprietary or exclusivity claims without demonstrating them.

**Non-equivalence:** A “revealed” price is not part of the creative metaphor; prices, allergens, items and order states should be explicit from the beginning. The metaphor must never be used to hide essential information.

## 4 — Six-lane generative stress test

| Lane | Its unique responsibility | Candidate expression from this thesis | Anti-repetition / utility limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand arrival | Make the food brand recognizable and emotionally compelling | Contrast an intact, recognizable product with a real physical interior; editorial rhythm of quiet/wide and dense/close | NO generic full-screen hero plus obligatory scroll cut reveal; immediate skip/direct menu |
| Food editorial / discovery | Clarify what is distinctive about meals | Ingredient/texture photostories; measured context vs cut; some products need whole-only imagery | Different treatment by food type; no mask on every product |
| Product choice | Find and understand exact item, form, size and add-ons | Plain precise details; optionally a controlled whole/cut comparison when genuinely informative | Utility first, readable without any animation |
| Offers / campaign entry | Honor a social promise | Graphic energy and authentic hero food, with terms visibly readable | No conceal/reveal mechanic for deadlines, exclusions or price |
| Cart / status | Correct ordering and honest fulfillment | Deliberately calm, literal typography and controls; true saved/confirmed/preparing states | Almost zero decorative motion; no invented delivery progress |
| Packaging / handoff | Translate brand into physical evidence | Research actual wrap folds, printed surfaces, carry/open gestures; possible contrast-based printed treatment | No fictional process, no final packaging before original measurements and permissions |

**Cross-lane rule:** A single creative world must have distinct expressions. Six pages with identical red-on-black headlines, cutaway photographs and GSAP reveals would fail. This explicitly transfers the *anti-repetition discipline* from HILTECH, not its appearance.

## 5 — Proposed formal study vocabulary (NO font/color/design choices locked)

These are **experimental axes**, not art direction specifications.

1. **Density:** restrained outer frame ↔ dense real material; test legibility before character.
2. **Scale:** whole product ↔ true macro texture ↔ human-held portion reference. Do not exaggerate.
3. **Cropping:** product exterior vs internal detail; cropping must not misstate filling quantity.
4. **Hierarchy:** display typography can contrast compression and openness; transactional type stays legible. Font families and Arabic letterforms remain undecided.
5. **Color roles:** derive from verified food, real packaging and local context rather than automatically inheriting legacy red/yellow/black.
6. **Motion:** opening/exposure is one editorial verb; static and touch/keyboard-controlled alternatives come first. No obligatory scroll theater on checkout.
7. **Photography:** real ordinary servings: intact, genuine cut, hand-to-scale, wrapper, one relevant cooking/action view. Record dimensions, date, recipe confirmation and authorization.
8. **Tone:** appetizing, local, specific; no forced high-end Italian stereotype or generic street-food slang.
9. **Navigation:** separate quick exact-item and campaign entry from exploratory discovery, independent of visual signature.

## 6 — Failure tests

- **Originality gate:** Show the test without Tuscanini logo. Does it look indistinguishable from any stuffed-crepe restaurant? If yes, revisit product/place-specific material.
- **Truth gate:** Show the actual served portion beside the creative photo. Is there any false impression of size, topping, contents or preparation?
- **Meaning gate:** Is the still + precise description sufficient? If yes, motion is optional, not a priority.
- **Range gate:** Produce three different treatments—food editorial, one physical surface, one campaign—without repeating a single mask/layout.
- **Tempo gate:** Can someone find a named food and order on weak mobile network without entering the editorial journey?
- **Distinct categories gate:** Does the concept work respectfully with pizza, pasta and dessert as well as cut-open crepe? If no, limit it to the category where it's truthful.
- **Accessibility gate:** Content works with image alt, keyboard, touch and reduced-motion; key information visible without animation.
- **Evidence gate:** Never infer recipe identity, sales ranking, identity ownership or staff ritual from the legacy repository and screenshots.

**If these gates fail, this working thesis must be retired or narrowed—not defended with louder animation.**

## 7 — A small executable creative lab (the next *actual* visual experiment)

### EXP-06A / THREE CONTRAST STUDIES — deliverable: 3 art-directed compositions, not 3 websites

All three should use the **same current genuine ordinary-sized food serving** when possible; until that is available, use the supplied screenshot only for private rough study and label it as lower-resolution/partial.

**Study 1 — Still / factual**
- Actual whole product, cut interior, exact item, real size and ingredients; no transition.
- What we measure: correct product understanding; compositional quality.

**Study 2 — Editorial tension**
- Contrast calm outer geometry with dense, authentic interior; test crop, spacing and type rhythm *without copying a reference layout*.
- What we measure: does the image language have recognizable authored character beyond “food is full”?

**Study 3 — User-controlled disclosure**
- Same photo material, one simple click/tap/keyboard comparison or short mask reveal with static equivalent.
- What we measure: whether disclosure adds information, user control and delight over Studies 1/2, at tolerable performance cost.

**Not yet authorized:** page mockups for every route, full UI kit, final brand book, 3D production, new Google/Vercel deployment.

### EXP-06B / PHYSICAL CONTRAST CHECK
- Choose one real wrap/box, label materials and dimensions, shoot closed/open states.
- Ask whether “inside/out” is actually a useful physical system or a web-only trick.
- Without the physical material, record **pending**, do not invent packaging.

### EXP-06C / PRODUCT PATH CHECK
- With verified menu items, test text-only routes: known name, known form, promotional entry.
- Capture accuracy, errors/backtracks and item understanding; do not declare a winner based on desk assumptions.

## 10 — Evidence that would materially change this direction

1. Normal portions do **not** look like promotional cuts: revise photographic premise.
2. The restaurant's most recognized differentiator is something else (staff personality, unusual naming, cooking gesture, community experience): reopen the thesis.
3. The actual packaging/handoff has a strong brand-specific gesture: promote Candidate C for further testing.
4. Different food forms do share a rigorously consistent, customer-recognized signature recipe: promote Candidate A beyond a taxonomy curiosity.
5. Rapid usability feedback shows reveal interaction adds nothing: retain still-photo contrast or find another creative verb.

**Field materials eventually needed:** current menu and recipe descriptions, 3 authentic product captures, front-counter/packaging documentation, selected customer/staff observations and permission to use imagery.
